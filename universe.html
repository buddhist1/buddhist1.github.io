Buddha's modern description of the universe from 2500 years ago

In Cūḷanikāsutta (source) , Ananda talked to Buddha on how far he can make his voice heard. In response, Buddha gave a surprisingly similar description of the universe with the modern understanding

“Ānanda, a galaxy extends a thousand times as far as the moon and sun revolve and the shining ones light up the quarters. In that galaxy there are a thousand moons, a thousand suns, a thousand Sinerus king of mountains, a thousand Black Plum Tree Lands, a thousand lands west of Godāvarī, a thousand lands north of Kuru, a thousand lands east of Videha, four thousand oceans, four thousand great kings, a thousand realms of the gods of the four great kings, a thousand realms of the gods of the thirty-three, of the gods of Yama, of the joyful gods, of the gods who love to create, of the gods who control what is created by others, and a thousand realms of divinity. This is called a thousandfold lesser world system, a ‘galaxy’.

A world system that extends for a thousand galaxies is called a millionfold middling world system, a ‘galactic cluster’.

A world system that extends for a thousand galactic clusters is called a billionfold great world system, a ‘galactic supercluster’.

Obviously, the terms 'galaxy', 'galactic cluster' and 'galactic supercluster' are just modern translation of this sutta. The original words Buddha used was 'Sahassī Cūḷanikā Loka-dhātu', 'Dvisahassī Middhamā Loka-dhātu', and Tisahassī Mahāsahassī Loka-dhātu, each describing things exponentially bigger than the previous one.

The sutta also used seemingly strange descriptions like 'thousand Black Plum Tree Lands', 'a thousand lands west of Godāvarī' but this is most likely because there were no good words to describe massive celestial objects. Mind you telescope weren't invented until thousand of years later.

Compared to the false understanding of Geocentric model (the world revolves around the earth) that was widely accepted in the past for over 2000 years, this description of the universe from 2500 years ago is really groundbreaking because it indirectly implies that the world doesn't revolve around the earth, but the earth is just a small part of it. It also described the world is filled with massive amount of other celestial objects like galaxies, galactic clusters and superclusters that weren't even understood until Edwin Hubble's observation in 1924 using a massive telescope.
